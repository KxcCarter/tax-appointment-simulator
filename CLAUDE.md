# Income Tax Appointment Simulator: Design & Build Brief

> **For Claude, starting a new session:** This brief is the source of truth for the game. Build **the first draft of the Waiting Room level only** (Milestones 1–5 below). Do not build the Accountant's Office yet. When you're done, deliver a playable build and list anything you assumed or left as a placeholder. Where this brief says *(proposal)*, it's a suggestion: implement it, but keep it easy to change.

---

## 1. Concept

A short first-person 3D game about attending a tax appointment. The tone is Kafkaesque, with very, very, very dry humor: the comedy comes from pacing, silence, indifference and bureaucratic absurdity. Nothing is played for winks. The world takes itself completely seriously.

The waiting-room sequence is slow, quiet and frustrating on purpose. It then abruptly turns into a ridiculous physics brawl with flopping ragdolls. That contrast is the core joke.

## 2. Player experience: Waiting Room level

### 2.1 Arrival
- The player starts just inside the entrance of the tax preparer's office, facing the waiting room.
- There are **5 other people** already seated and waiting.
- An **analog clock** on the wall reads **1:55**. It ticks quietly; the tick is positional audio, louder near the clock.
- There's a **front desk** with a **receptionist** (very fat, owner's decision; her build is `config.characters.receptionist.proportions`) playing solitaire on her computer. Mouse clicks are audible near the desk.

### 2.2 The receptionist exchange (pacing is everything)
Walking up to the desk and looking at her shows an interaction prompt. Dialogue choices appear on screen. Each attempt is gated by a timer, so the player is forced to wait.

| Beat | Player option that advances the story | Receptionist response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I'm here for my appointment." | Looks at the player for a moment (about 2 s of silence), then turns back to her solitaire. Says nothing. |
| — | *(Dialogue options are unavailable for about 10–15 s; only mouse clicks are heard.)* | |
| 2 | "I'm here for my appointment." | Ignores the player for a few seconds (clicks continue). Without looking: **"Sshhh, momma's got to play her game."** |
| — | *(Another wait, about 10–15 s.)* | |
| 3 | "I'm here for my appointment at 2:00 PM." | Without looking: **"Take a number and have a seat."** |

- **Optional follow-up (added by the owner):** right after "Take a number and have a seat.", choices open by themselves (no E press needed):
  - Player: "But I have an appointment at 2." → Receptionist: **"What time is it now?"**
  - Player chooses either the **exact time on the wall clock** (e.g. "It's 1:57.") or **"It's 2:00."**
    - "It's 2:00." → she glances over her shoulder at the wall clock, then: **"No it's not."** The question stands; the same two choices come back. *Exception:* if the clock really reads 2:00, "It's 2:00." is the true answer: it's the only choice shown, and she moves on to "And what time is your appointment?"
    - The exact time → **"And what time is your appointment?"** → Player: "My appointment is at 2:00." → **"Well it's not 2:00 yet, is it?"** → **"Take a seat and wait."**
      - The clock runs in real time, so her reply depends on what it reads when she answers. **On time = the clock reads 2:00 to 2:02** (`config.appointment.onTimeMinutes`; the owner may narrow it):
        - before 2:00: "Well it's not 2:00 yet, is it?" → "Take a seat and wait." (as above)
        - 2:00 to 2:02: the **check-in runaround** (owner's design): **"Did you check in already?"** → "No." / "That's what I'm trying to do right now." → **"You gotta check in first."** The choices close; the player has to press E again: "How do I check in?" / "Can you check me in, please?" → she ignores it: **"Ooo, look at them cards fly, hoo hoo!"** → the same two questions come straight back → **"You gotta sign the check-in sheet."** (she doesn't hand it over). For about 10 s (`timings.reception.newGameWait`) nothing works; pressing E gets **"Hold up, momma gotta start a new game..."** (a beat's `lockedResponse`). Then: "Can I check in for my appointment, please?" → **"Did you sign the check-in sheet?"** → "No." → she turns and looks at him: **"You can't go to your appointment until you check in."** → "Okay, so can I check in now?" → **"Gotta sign the sheet."** (back to her game). The choices close; press E again: "Please, can you just sign me in?" → **"Did you sign the check-in sheet?"**, and without looking up she slides the clipboard across the counter → "No." → **"Everybody gotta sign the check-in sheet. Did you sign it?"** (beats `checkin_already`, `checkin_how`, `checkin_how_again`, `checkin_can_i`, `checkin_sheet`, `checkin_okay`, `checkin_please`, `checkin_offered`).
      - **No pen (owner's design):** the clipboard on the counter can be used ("[E] Sign Check-in Sheet") → "You don't have a pen." Then the player can ask her "Do you have a pen?" She ignores it: **"Mamma gotta get her some coffee..."**, gets up, walks through the **EMPLOYEES ONLY** door behind the desk (into a small back room), is gone ~30 s (`timings.reception.coffeeAway`; meanwhile the player can knock, chat and use things as usual), and comes back to her seat with a cup of coffee. Then: "Do you have a pen I can borrow?" → **"No."** → "How am I supposed to check in for my appointment then?" → **"You can check-in online. What time is your appointment?"** → "2:00." / "It's supposed to be right now." → she looks at the clock, back at her computer, takes a sip of coffee: **"You're late for your appointment so the system won't let you sign in online, you're gonna have to fill in the check-in sheet."** The player can ask for a pen again. **The second time she refuses** ("No."), she calls the next number: **"Number 848! Our 2:00 was a no-show so you can go on ahead."** Now Serving chimes and jumps to 848, and the exchange is over.
      - **A newcomer (owner's design):** two seconds after she's back at her desk with the coffee, the entrance door swings open (there's a corridor outside) and a new person (a "bro", owner's redesign: a white tank top (a wife-beater), navy cargo shorts, sneakers, sunglasses) walks in, takes ticket **848** from the dispenser and sits in a free chair (`config.newcomer`).
        - 2:03 or later: **"You're late. You'll have to take a number and have a seat."** *(proposal: the late route rejoins the story, beat `late_wait`)* After `config.timings.reception.lateWait` seconds he can come back to the desk: "I've been sitting there for ages. Can I at least check in?" (or, rudely, "I'm not taking a number. I had an appointment. Check me in.") → **"Did you check in already?"** → the check-in runaround (sheet, pen, coffee, 848...) as for an on-time player.
      - The player can instead say "My appointment is right now." Unless the clock reads 2:00 to 2:02, she replies **"We don't have any appointments scheduled for {the time on the clock}."** and the same two choices come back. If it is 2:00 to 2:02, she answers as for an on-time player (the check-in sheet).
      - After "Well it's not 2:00 yet, is it? … Take a seat and wait." (or "(Say nothing)" to the first question) the player can walk back up later and say "But I have an appointment at 2." again (beat `protest_again`), so a patient player can reach the check-in sheet once it's 2:00.
  - The player can also say nothing and walk away; pressing E on her later picks the conversation up where it stopped.
- **During a wait (decided by the owner):** the player can still walk up and press E, but **no dialogue options appear**. Beat data has a `lockedOptions` list (empty for now) so recorded lines can be added there later without engine changes.

- Each beat also offers 1–2 other options (e.g. "Hello?", "Excuse me.", "(Say nothing)"). These get flat or no responses and don't advance the story. *(proposal: options that are nearly identical to each other are part of the joke.)*
- **Said lines go away (owner's decision):** within a beat, a line the player picks that doesn't move the story on (a flat reply, silence, or a jump back to the same beat such as "It's 2:00." → "No it's not.") is not offered again on that visit. Exception: a beat where no option moves the story on is waiting for the player to do something in the world (e.g. sign the clipboard), so its options stay. An option can opt out with `repeatable: true` ("stays" in the Scene Desk). Coming back to a beat later offers everything again.
- **Variations (owner's request):** any line can have several versions (`text: ['...', '...']` on a `say`; "+ variations" in the Scene Desk). Each time it runs one of them is said: in the order written, then at random once all have been used (`vary: 'random'` = random, never twice in a row; `'any'` = truly random, repeats allowed; `'cycle'` = in order, round and round). Useful for loops like `checkin_offered`.
- Timings must be in a config file so they're easy to tune.

### 2.3 Taking a number and waiting
- A ticket dispenser sits on or near the desk. The player takes a ticket. **The ticket can be taken at any time, even before the receptionist says so, but it doesn't help:** she still ignores the first two attempts (decided by the owner). *(proposal: the ticket number is absurdly high, e.g. **847**, while a "Now Serving" display shows a low number such as **12**.)*
- The player can sit in an empty chair (interact to sit and stand) or wander around.
- **The wait counts no matter what** the player does (sitting, standing, wandering) (decided by the owner).
- **Placeholder for content not yet designed:** the time between taking a number and being called. For the first draft, use a configurable wait (default **5 minutes**, owner's decision, so the clock passes 2:00 before the call) with small ambient beats: NPC idle animations, a sigh, someone checking their watch, someone coughing too often, the clock ticking. **The clock always runs in real time** (decided by the owner).
- **The waiting people (first draft, Milestone 3):** each has one idle personality: the **cougher** (coughs too often), the **watch-checker** (checks his watch, then the clock, sighs; has a briefcase), the **reader** (reads a magazine, turns pages), the **leg-jiggler** (arms crossed, knee bouncing, long exhales at the ceiling), and the **sitter** (a bit of a seductress, owner's request: an hourglass figure, a fitted wine-red dress with a plunging neckline and a pencil skirt, dark stockings, red heels, long hair, a knowing half-smile (expression `sultry`); barely moves, shifts occasionally). All glance at the clock now and then. Their eyes (not heads) follow the player up close; everyone looks over when the player takes a ticket; sitting right next to someone makes them lean away. **Small talk (designed by the owner):** the player can talk to each of them: "Hello."/"Excuse me." → their greeting ("Hello." / "Hi." / "Hey.") → "What time is your appointment?" → "I don't have an appointment." / "My appointment is at 3:00." / "...at 5:30." → "Okay, thank you." (ends) or "How long have you been waiting?" → people without an appointment answer anything from an hour to since yesterday. Lines live in `src/data/npcConversations.ts`. While talking they turn to the player and stop fidgeting. Only one conversation runs at a time (the receptionist's call waits for it). After the call, nobody talks to the player.
- **At the call** the Now Serving display chimes and jumps to the ticket number, and all five heads turn to the player one by one and stay there, annoyed. That's where the brawl (Milestone 4) takes over. If the player is mid-conversation with the receptionist, the call waits for the current line to finish, then ends the exchange.
- **The radio (owner's request):** after exactly 1 minute of play (pauses don't count; `config.radio.at`), one of the waiting people (the sitter, from the opposite side of the room; `config.radio.who`) gets up, walks across the room to a small radio on the side table, turns it on and sits back down. It plays `look_to_the_sky.mp3` (supplied by the owner, in `src/audio/`) very, very softly, slightly tinny, on repeat, until the player can leave for the accountant (`doorOpen`). It keeps playing through the brawl. **The player can turn the radio off** ("[E] Turn off the radio"). The sitter gets up immediately and goes back to turn it on again, every time, angrier each time (annoyed → angry → furious), walking faster and faster (`config.npc.anger`), stomping, and glaring at the player on her way back. If the radio is switched off while she's walking back, she turns straight round. From the second time on she mutters as she gets up: "Rude...", "Seriously??", "No way...", "Really dude?", "Oooh no, nuh-uh!", "Son of a..." (in order, then at random; `radioComplaints` in `src/data/npcConversations.ts`). **She gets violent (owner's design):** from the 3rd time the radio is turned off she walks to the player and attacks first, then turns the radio back on: 3rd slap, 4th shove, 5th punch, 6th kick, 7th a slap-shove-punch combo, 8th+ she picks up the playing radio, hits the player with it, and puts it back (`config.npc.violence`, `config.npc.attacks`). Hits are feel only for now (knockback, camera shake, sound; no health). The player can't fight back until after her kick; then fists come up and left-click punches. **The onlookers (owner's idea):** the first time the player's punch lands on her, everyone else reacts as if she did absolutely nothing wrong and he's a violent maniac: heads snap round, a shocked face, then they stare at him, appalled, for good (they stop fidgeting and won't make small talk any more: [E] on one of them gets a cold brush-off instead, e.g. "Don't you talk to me.", "Keep walking, buddy." *(proposal; `appalledReplies` in `src/data/npcConversations.ts`)*). Lines, in order: Woman with magazine "Oh my God." → Older man "He hit her." → Man with briefcase "She was listening to the radio." → Young man "What is wrong with you, man?" → (the newcomer, if he's arrived) "Unbelievable." → Woman with magazine "Somebody do something." → the receptionist, without looking up from her solitaire: **"Sir, I'm going to have to ask you to calm down, please."** *(proposal: the lines; edit `onlookers` in `src/data/npcConversations.ts`)*. She flinches when hit and, after 8 punches (twice the usual before the brawl, owner's request: `config.radio.toughness`), falls down, which ends the radio war. **When the brawl starts she gets back up** (owner's request: `config.radio.wakeForBrawl`): out of a crouch, fully recovered (bruises and all), and joins in; that knockdown then no longer counts towards the 4. The song resumes where it would be by now, like a live station.
- **"My eyes are up HERE!" (owner's request):** whenever the sitter is up and walking (the radio, the radio war, the brawl, the argument), if the player looks at her (within `angle` of her chest, for `lookFor` seconds), she stops, turns on him, furious, and says, in order then at random: **"Are you looking at my breasts??"** (hands cupped under her bust, palms up, as if presenting it; owner's change), **"Hey perv, my eyes are up HERE!"** (two fingers jabbing at her eyes, the other hand on her hip), **"This creep is looking at my breasts!!"** (pointing at him), then carries on where she was going. Not while she's carrying the radio, and not while anyone else is talking; then not again for `cooldown` seconds (`config.npc.eyesUp`; lines and gestures: `eyesUp` in `src/data/npcConversations.ts`; poses `coverChest`, `pointEyes`, `pointAt` in `src/characters/walker.js`).
- **The gamer (owner's character):** after 90 s of play (`config.gamer.at`; pauses don't count; `?gamer=10` in the URL for testing) a huge, morbidly obese young man walks in through the entrance (a charcoal T-shirt stretched over his belly, cargo shorts, a cap on backwards, headphones round his neck, stubble), straight past the ticket dispenser *(proposal: he doesn't take a ticket, so 848 stays the newcomer's)*, and sits in the first free chair of `config.gamer.seatPreference` (7, between the young man and the woman with the magazine). He carries a family-size bag of chips and an energy drink; seated, the bag sits on his left knee and the can on his right. Every few seconds (`config.gamer.snack`) he eats a handful of chips (rustle, crunch, chewing), takes a long sip (gulps, sometimes a burp), or upends the bag over his open mouth for the crumbs. **His belly bounces when he walks** (also when he sits down or is hit): his belly, chest and jowls wobble on a spring (`jiggle` in `config.characters.builds.gamer`), and he walks slower with a deeper bounce, a waddle and heavy footsteps (`config.gamer.gait`). He has no lines yet. He joins the brawl like everyone else (with the can still in his hand); knocked down, the bag and the can fly.
- When the wait is over, the receptionist calls the player: *(proposal)* "Eight hundred forty-seven. He'll see you now." The Now Serving display jumps to 847.

## 3. Technical approach

### 3.1 Stack
- **Three.js** for rendering.
- **Rapier** (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`, which embeds the WASM so no separate file is needed) for physics, player movement and ragdolls.
- **Vite** dev server and bundler, plain JavaScript (ES modules). **Exception (owner's decision): scene data is TypeScript** (`src/data/waitingRoomBeats.ts`, types in `src/director/types.ts`); `npm run typecheck` checks it. Vite runs .ts directly.
- **No external art or sound assets in the first draft**, with exceptions chosen by the owner: the radio song `src/audio/look_to_the_sky.mp3` and the clock's tick-tock `src/audio/clock_tick.mp3` (a 2 s recording, restarted on every even second of the game clock). All geometry is built from primitives in code, and all sounds are synthesized with the Web Audio API (clock tick, mouse clicks, punches, thuds, voice blips). **Recorded sound effects (owner, in progress):** any synthesized sound can be replaced by dropping a file named after it into `src/audio/sfx/` (names and where each plays: `src/audio/soundList.js`, also listed in that folder's README; numbered versions such as `hit_punch_1.wav` are picked at random). Per sound, `config.audio.sounds` can keep the synthesized one (`synth`) or play both (`both`); `config.audio.fileVolume` sets loudness. `npm run sounds` lists what's there, what's missing and misnamed files.
- **Generated sound effects (owner):** `npm run sfxgen` (`scripts/sfxgen.mjs`) makes sound effects with ElevenLabs (text to sound effects) from prompts in the script, three takes each, as trimmed WAVs normalised to the same peak (`--only <name>`, `--force`, `--dry`, `--normalize`). Made so far: `hit_punch`, `hit_slap` (also the karate chop), `hit_kick`, `hit_bat`, `hit_chair`, and `swing_bat`, a new sound: the bat swooshes as it comes round, hit or miss (`swingSound` in `config.weapons`). **The owner's own recordings are never replaced** (e.g. `hit_bat_1`, `hit_kick_1`, `swing_bat_1`, `hit_punch_1`–`4`): the script only touches files it made, listed in `src/audio/sfx/generated.json`. **Small things landing** (the bat, radio, keyboard, the gamer's can, hats, glasses: lighter than `config.physics.impact.debrisBelow` kg) use the owner's `debris` recordings; heavier things still `prop_thud`.
- **Recorded voices (owner):** voiced with ElevenLabs (key in `.env.local`, which git ignores): the receptionist (`u0REnIJvUgcGQYW2Ux8K`), the woman in red / `sitter` (`wkqe33083YLBV35we7yB`), the older man / `cougher` (`MKlLqCItoCkvdhrxgtLv`), the young man / `jiggler` (`mrQhZWGbb2k9qWJb5qeA`), the `newcomer` (`Cx1u6YPIa1SPiAbYj3gJ`), the man in the suit with the briefcase / `watchChecker` (`3y1FmEGNzXJhlyyCO6Kr`) and the woman with the magazine / `reader` (`ejl43bbp2vjkAFGSmAMa`); the gamer still has blips. `npm run voices` (`scripts/voices.mjs`, voices in `VOICES`) collects every line each of them says from the data files (brawl shouts, which go to whoever's standing, for all of them), generates the ones without a recording into `src/audio/voice/<who>/` (`lines.json` maps text to file), and lists recordings of lines no longer said (`--prune` deletes them); `--dry` lists and counts without generating, `--only "text"` / `--force` regenerate. Lines with the clock time or ticket number in them are generated for the values they can take (1:55 to 2:30; tickets 1,694 to 27,104). A line said with exactly a recording's text plays it from the speaker instead of the blips, and the text types out with it (`config.audio.voices`); edited lines fall back to blips until `npm run voices` is run again. **Directing a line (owner):** `DIRECTIONS` in the script replaces what's sent for that one line (e.g. `[scared]Oh Lawd he violent!!` on `eleven_v3`, whose audio tags set the delivery, stability 0 = most expressive); the text on screen is unchanged. Every request sent is kept in `src/audio/voice/<who>/sent.json`, and changing a line's direction re-records it on the next run. Years are read as years ("nineteen seventy-nine"); lines with no words ("...") keep their blips. Other characters get a voice by adding them to `VOICES` in the script. **The owner's own recordings are never replaced or deleted** (owner's rule): all of the **receptionist's and the reader's** lines were re-rendered by hand (`OWNER_VOICES` in the script: only new lines are generated for them, never re-recorded, not even with `--force`, and never pruned). For everyone else, only files fingerprinted in `<who>/made.json` (the ones the script made) can be replaced; any file swapped in by hand is kept.
- **Fallback if npm installs are unavailable in the session:** build a single HTML file that loads Three.js and Rapier from a CDN via an import map. Same architecture, fewer files.

### 3.2 Visual style
- Simple and comical, with a 1980s/90s look in the set dressing (not in the rendering). Characters and props are built from boxes, capsules, cylinders and spheres, like low-poly toy figures.
- Flat colors with `MeshLambertMaterial`, or `MeshToonMaterial` with a 3-step gradient.
- **No pixelated / low-resolution render.** Render at full resolution with antialiasing and smooth texture filtering. (A retro render was tried and dropped by the owner.)
- **Palette:** a 1980s/90s tax office: beige walls, brown carpet, mint and avocado accents, wood-veneer desk, slightly greenish fluorescent light, a beige CRT computer.
- **All look-related settings live in one file, `src/theme.js`:** palette colors, a material factory (e.g. `theme.material("wallBeige")`), lighting setup (types, colors, intensities, shadows), fog and background, and any post-processing. Character colors and accessories come from palettes defined there too. No other file may hard-code a color, create a material directly, or set up lights. That way the whole art style can change by editing `theme.js` alone (e.g. swap `MeshLambertMaterial` for `MeshToonMaterial` in one place).
- **Realistic or cartoon characters (owner):** the cast are realistic MakeHuman models by default (`theme.look.realistic`; built with `tools/makehuman/`). The cartoon figures are kept as an option: the player picks **CHARACTERS: Realistic / Cartoon** on the title screen (remembered in the browser; switching reloads the game), and `?realistic=0` / `?realistic=1` in the URL override it. Either way the cartoon rig runs underneath (the realistic body copies its pose; its parts are the hitboxes), so gameplay is the same.
- **Character style: `natural`** (owner asked for more realistic, less blocky bodies; proposal, replacing `figure`). The figure's expressive face on a more lifelike body: a head with a jaw, sloping shoulders, a chest, a waist and a seat, muscle-shaped limbs with narrow wrists and ankles and smooth elbows and knees, hands with fingers and a thumb, shoes with a heel and toe, trousers that hang straight, a shirt front and tie that follow the chest, fitted hair and caps, belts (`src/characters/partShapes.js`, sizes in `config.characters.styleProportions.natural`). Same joints and sizes as every other style, so animation, hit detection and ragdolls are unchanged. Set by `theme.look.characterStyle`; the others (`figure`, the previous choice, and `blocky`, `rounded`, `capsule`, `soft`, `sculpted`) remain available for comparison via `?charstyle=` in the URL.
- **Faces can show expression** (`figure` style): eyeballs with gaze, blinking eyelids, independent eyebrows, and a mouth that can frown, smirk, smile, open, and move while talking. Expressions are named presets in `config.characters.expressions`; beat data can switch them with `{ do: 'face', who, expression }`. A scripted face wins over the expressions animations pick: on the receptionist it holds until the script changes it; on the waiting people until their conversation ends; `expression: 'default'` hands the face back to the character.
- **Fixed early (expensive to change later):** characters are built from **separate primitive body parts** (the toy-like look), because animation, hit detection and ragdolls depend on that construction. Body proportions (chunky, slightly big-headed figures) and room scale are defined as constants in `config.js` so they can still be re-tuned.
- Room contents: a printed notice on the wall behind the receptionist, **"Please be sure to arrive at least 5 minutes before your scheduled appointment."** (owner's request; readable from the counter: "You were here at 1:55."), rows of chairs along the walls, a small side table with magazines, a potted plant, a water cooler, an umbrella stand, the Now Serving display, the clock, the ticket dispenser, the front desk with CRT computer, keyboard and mouse, a door marked **"ACCOUNTANT – PRIVATE"**, and an **"EMPLOYEES ONLY"** door behind the reception desk leading to a small back room.

### 3.3 Player controller (first person)
- Pointer-lock mouse look, WASD movement, Shift to walk slightly faster *(proposal: the player can never truly run; office decorum)*, E to interact.
- A Rapier **kinematic character controller** with a capsule collider, so the player collides with walls and furniture and can push light props.
- Interaction uses a raycast from the camera centre and shows a small prompt, e.g. "[E] Talk", "[E] Sit", "[E] Take number", "[E] Pick up umbrella".
- Seated mode locks the camera to the chair with limited look-around.

### 3.4 Characters and ragdolls
- **Fixed cast (decided by the owner):** the five waiting people are always the same characters with the same design, defined explicitly (looks in `theme.js`, seats and personalities in `src/data/waitingRoomCast.js`), not randomized per load.
- Every humanoid (NPCs and receptionist) uses one parametric **primitive rig**: pelvis, torso, head, upper and lower arms, upper and lower legs. Clothing colors, height, hats (fedora, cap), hair and accessories are randomized per NPC so they read as distinct people.
- **Two modes per character:**
  1. **Animated (alive):** limbs are driven procedurally in code (sitting, fidgeting, standing up, walking, punching, flinching). Rapier bodies are *kinematic* and follow the animation, so hits can be detected.
  2. **Ragdoll (knocked down):** switch every body part to a *dynamic* Rapier rigid body, connected with joints (spherical joints for shoulders and hips, revolute joints for elbows and knees, all with sensible limits). Copy current velocities over, then apply the hit impulse at the contact point.
- Tune mass, damping and joint limits for **maximum comedic flop** while keeping limbs from passing through each other or exploding.
- Props (chairs, magazines, plant pot, water-cooler bottle, umbrella stand) are dynamic bodies, so ragdolls can knock them over.

### 3.5 Combat
- Attack hit detection uses a Rapier **shape cast** (sphere or capsule) in front of the camera, sized by the attack or weapon's reach.
- A weapon table in config sets `damage`, `reach`, `swingTime`, `knockbackImpulse` and `sound` for fist, kick, bat, umbrella and keyboard.
- **Game feel:** hit-stop (about 60 ms freeze on impact), small camera shake, a white flash on the hit NPC, and an exaggerated knockback impulse. *(proposal: the keyboard sheds a few loose keycap physics bodies on each hit.)*
- **NPC combat AI:** approach the player, wind up (a readable telegraph), swing, then a short cooldown. They flinch and stagger when hit. They don't all attack at once; 2–3 are active at a time while the others shout from the edges.

### 3.6 NPC state machine
`Seated → IdleFidget → Indignant (stand + shout) → Approach → WindUp → Attack → Cooldown → (Stunned) → KnockedDown (ragdoll)`

Receptionist: `PlayingSolitaire ⇄ GlanceAtPlayer → (calls player's number)`. She does not take part in the brawl. *(proposal: she keeps playing solitaire throughout the fight.)*

### 3.7 Scene director (beat system)
The narrative is **data-driven**. The level is a list of beats in `src/data/waitingRoomBeats.ts`, and a director steps through them. Each beat can define its trigger, available dialogue options, responses (lines, animations, sounds), delays, and `nextUnlockAfter` timers. Changing the pacing should never require touching engine code. An option's `actions` play after its line is said, both with `goto` (before moving) and with `advance` (before the beat's `onAdvance`). `trigger.target` is whose [E] reopens a beat's choices if the player closes them, not who speaks; keep it `'receptionist'` for the main scene. It can be a list, e.g. `['receptionist', 'reader']` for a line asked of the reader (`argument_4`, `argument_5`; owner's request), so [E] on either opens the choices (the Scene Desk takes them comma-separated). **Aggression (owner's request, the stage set; nothing reacts yet):** every option can have an `aggression`, 0 polite (the default), 1 curt, 2 rude, 3 hostile (a menu on each option in the Scene Desk). When one is picked, the director sets `state.lastAggression` to it and adds it to `state.temper` before anything is said, so its actions, the beat's `onAdvance` and later beats can branch on it (`{ do: 'if', test: (s) => s.lastAggression >= 2, ... }`, or `s.temper` for how hot-headed he's been all along).

Example shape:
```js
{
  id: "reception_1",
  trigger: { type: "interact", target: "receptionist" },
  options: [
    { text: "I'm here for my appointment.", advance: true },
    { text: "Hello?", response: null },
  ],
  onAdvance: [
    { do: "anim", who: "receptionist", name: "lookAtPlayer", duration: 2 },
    { do: "anim", who: "receptionist", name: "resumeSolitaire" },
  ],
  next: "reception_2",
  nextUnlockAfter: 12,
}
```

### 3.8 UI
- An HTML/CSS overlay on top of the canvas.
- Dialogue box at the bottom: speaker name plus line, with text revealed character by character with soft blips.
- Choice list with number keys 1–4 or mouse click.
- Crosshair dot, interaction prompt, held-weapon name, player health (a subtle bar), and a knockdown counter during the brawl ("Knocked down: 2 / 4").
- Title screen: **INCOME TAX APPOINTMENT SIMULATOR** with "Click to begin." Pause menu on Esc.
- Font: a monospace or system font styled to look like an old office terminal (no external font downloads).

### 3.9 Audio (synthesized)
- **Clock tick:** positional, quiet, one per second.
- **Mouse clicks:** positional at the desk, irregular rhythm, with an occasional double-click.
- Low fluorescent-light hum (room tone).
- Footsteps; chair creak when sitting.
- Dialogue voice blips with a different pitch per character.
- Combat: punch thwack, kick thud, bat crack, umbrella "fwump", keyboard clatter, ragdoll body thuds based on impact velocity.

### 3.10 Global game state
A single state object holds `phase` (`arrival | reception | waiting | brawl | doorOpen`), `ticketNumber`, `nowServing`, `timesIgnored`, `knockdowns`, `playerHealth`, `heldWeapon` and `clockTime`. Later levels will read from this state.
